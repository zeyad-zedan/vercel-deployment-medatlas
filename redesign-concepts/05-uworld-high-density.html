ITEM ID: 20491 · LECTURE 1 (ORGANIZATION OF BODY) · DIFFICULTY: MEDIUM

A 28-year-old medical student is participating in a marathon on a hot day. After running 20 km without hydration, which of the following changes in extracellular fluid (ECF) and intracellular fluid (ICF) osmolarity and volume will be observed?

(A) Increased ECF volume, decreased ICF osmolarity
(B) Increased ECF osmolarity, decreased ICF volume (Hyperosmotic dehydration)
(C) Decreased ECF osmolarity, increased ICF volume
(D) Iso-osmotic contraction with no shift in ICF volume
Educational Objective:
Understand fluid volume contraction states. Sweat loss causes water to leave the ECF faster than salt, concentrating the ECF. Osmosis then pulls water from the ICF into the ECF, shrinking ICF volume.
Curriculum Reference
Slide 12 · Fluid Compartments Breakdown
Total Body Water: 60% BW
• ICF: 2/3 of TBW (40% BW)
• ECF: 1/3 of TBW (20% BW)
• Interstitial: 3/4 of ECF
• Plasma: 1/4 of ECF
Slide 14 · Darrow-Yannet Diagram
Volume contraction types:
1. Iso-osmotic (Diarrhea)
2. Hyper-osmotic (Sweating / Dehydration)
3. Hypo-osmotic (Adrenal insufficiency)
